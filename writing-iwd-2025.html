Quick review of thoughts on International Women’s Day 2025

Like many others, I wasn't born as a feminist, but as a human being with intellectual curiosity. I later became a feminist when I realized this gender role means much fewer opportunities, less credit for contributions, unreasonable emotional labor, and even personal safety concerns.

My engineering mindset popped up a question: Am I an outlier? To answer this question, over the years, I've read lots of stories and books about women across the world. Unfortunately, all I see is sufferings of diverse forms, no matter we females conform to the patriarchal norms or not, and the sufferings perpetuate across generations.

To find a solution, we need to locate the problem: “If you are in a position of power and influence, you can help. And if you’re not working for a solution, you’re part of the problem.” (The Power of Women, by Denis Mukwege). Being an unprivileged female, I've seen and experienced a whole spectrum of barriers. There are blatant sexists who show off their malice and prejudice. There are hypocrites who claim to support women's growth but constantly make micro-aggressions and blame women for feeling sensitive.

It's such a complicated issue that any individual will feel small and helpless. But the only thing we need to do is to acknowledge the contributions of women, and fully trust our potential to make a difference. I'm truly proud of being a female, for the traits we own, {resilience, pain tolerance, rationality, etc}.

This is a note for myself and anybody who might resonate.

“You can't sit on the lid of progress. If you do, you will be blown to pieces.”
